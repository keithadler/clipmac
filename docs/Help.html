Getting started

Clip for Mac lives in the menu bar. Everything you copy is kept for 30 days (adjustable), searchable, and one keystroke away. Press ⌥⌘V in any app to open the panel over what you're doing. Type to search, press ↩ to paste, esc to close.

The panel never takes focus away from the app you were using, so the item goes exactly where your cursor was.

Keyboard

Anywhere

⌥⌘VOpen or close the panel
⌥⇧⌘VPaste whatever is on the clipboard as plain text, without opening the panel
⌃⌥⌘VPaste the next item from the paste stack

All three can be changed or turned off in Settings › General. The app tells you if another app already owns a shortcut.

In the panel

TypeSearch. Words, fragments, and meaning ("that thing about the invoice") all work.
↑ ↓Move the selection
↩Paste the selected item
⌘↩Paste as plain text (formatting stripped)
⇧↩Add to the paste stack (see below)
⌘1…9Paste one of the top nine directly
⌘CCopy to the clipboard without pasting
⌘PPin or unpin
⌘⌫Delete the item (really deleted, no undo)
escClose

Right-click any item for the same actions plus Open Link, Show in Finder for files, and Forget everything from this app. You can also drag an item out of the panel into another app.

Pasting: two modes

Paste for you. When the Accessibility permission is granted, choosing an item pastes it into the app you were using. That permission is how macOS lets an app press ⌘V on your behalf; Clip for Mac uses it for nothing else. Grant it in Settings › Paste. Afterwards your previous clipboard is put back (adjustable delay, or off).

Copy only. Without that permission, choosing an item copies it and closes the panel; you press ⌘V. Everything else works the same. The panel footer tells you which mode you're in.

The paste stack

Press ⇧↩ on several items to queue them, then press ⌃⌥⌘V in a form to paste them one after another, oldest first. The menu bar shows how many are queued. The stack is cleared when the app quits.

What is never captured

  • Anything a password manager marks as concealed or transient (1Password, Bitwarden, Keychain Access, Strongbox, KeePassXC and others do this).
  • Anything copied while a password field has focus. The menu bar icon shows a lock while that is true.
  • Anything copied from an app on the exclusion list (Settings › Capture). It ships pre-filled with the common password managers; add any app.
  • Anything larger than the size cap (50 MB by default).

These are not settings. There is no switch that turns them off.

Items that still look like secrets (API keys, tokens, private keys, card numbers, password=…) are marked with a shield in the panel. They stay on your Mac and are masked before any optional cloud request. clipmac forget --sensitive deletes them all at once.

Pause

Pause capture from the menu bar for 10 minutes, 30 minutes, an hour, or until you resume. The icon changes while paused. Copying still works normally; it just isn't recorded.

Pins and snippets

Pinned items sit at the top of the panel and never expire. Give a pin a keyword in the preview pane and it becomes a snippet: clipmac snip <keyword> prints it, and the menu bar can export all keyworded pins as a macOS Text Replacement list. Drop that file onto System Settings › Keyboard › Text Replacements and macOS expands them as you type. Clip for Mac itself never watches what you type.

Ask your clipboard

Off unless you turn it on in Settings › Assist. Three levels, each stricter about what leaves the Mac:

The key is stored in your login Keychain. The provider bills their metered API, which is separate from a Claude Pro or ChatGPT Plus subscription. No proxy, no account, no relay through anyone's server.

Command line

clipmac is the same app, on your PATH after ./build-app.sh --install. Every command takes --json. Items are a position (1 = newest) or a stable id written as #42.

clipmac list --limit 20            clipmac search "invoice" --semantic
clipmac get 3                      clipmac copy #42 --plain
clipmac pin 3 --keyword sig        clipmac snip sig
clipmac pause 30m                  clipmac resume
clipmac forget #42 | --app com.example.thing | --sensitive
clipmac wipe --yes                 clipmac status
clipmac assist "what did I work on today" --local
clipmac assist log                 clipmac selftest

Scripts and Shortcuts can also drive the running app with URLs: clipmac://open, clipmac://search?q=invoice, clipmac://paste/42, clipmac://copy/42?plain=1, clipmac://pause?minutes=30, clipmac://resume, clipmac://settings. See man clipmac.

Privacy and storage

History lives in ~/Library/Application Support/Clip for Mac/history.db with images and rich text in a blobs folder beside it. Defaults: 30 days, 2,000 items, 1 GB; pinned items are exempt; deleting is real deletion. "Session only" in Settings › History keeps everything in memory instead.

Clip for Mac does not encrypt the database itself. Search can't index ciphertext, and half-encryption would imply more than it delivers. It relies on FileVault and warns you when FileVault is off. No account, no telemetry, no network unless you add your own key or click Check for Updates.

Troubleshooting

The panel doesn't open

Another app probably owns ⌥⌘V. The menu bar says so; pick a different shortcut in Settings › General. You can always open the panel from the menu bar.

Choosing an item doesn't paste

Accessibility isn't granted, so you're in copy-only mode: press ⌘V. To grant it, Settings › Paste › Grant Accessibility. After rebuilding from source the permission has to be granted again unless you ran ./make-local-identity.sh.

The wrong thing was pasted

A slow app read the clipboard after Clip for Mac had already restored your previous one. Raise the delay in Settings › Paste, or turn restoring off.

Something I copied isn't in the history

Check the menu bar: paused, or a password field had focus. Settings › Capture shows the reason the last item was skipped. Copies from excluded apps are never recorded.

Starting over

Settings › History › Forget everything, or clipmac wipe --yes. To remove the app entirely, delete it from Applications and the folder above; remove the API key from Keychain Access if you added one.
